Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for GCSE Chemistry. These are the resources I found most useful when revising for my exams. However, these are just my recommendations and opinions based off of what I found most useful. You may find other resources more useful for your revision!

1. Cognito

Cognito was one of my personal favourite websites for GCSE Chemistry. They provide clear animated explanations, quizzes and exam questions. Cognito is great for learning difficult concepts without making them unnecessarily complicated.

2. Physics and Maths Tutor (PMT)

PMT was a very helpful resource too. Topic by topic questions, notes, past papers and mark schemes. Particularly useful once you've learnt the content.

3. FreeScienceLessons

FreeScienceLessons provides short, specification-focused videos covering GCSE Chemistry topics. His videos may be slightly dull, but he covers the content accurately and quickly.

Top Tips:

· Learn the required practicals, including variables, method, results, errors and improvements.

· Practise calculations regularly.

· Learn the tests for ions and gases (carbon dioxide, chlorine, hydrogen and oxygen) and the expected observations.

· Practise interpreting graphs, tables and experimental data.

· Learn how to balance chemical equations - these can be easy marks if you know how to do them.

· Understand why reactions happen instead of memorising isolated facts.

· Make sure you know the difference between atoms, elements, compounds and mixtures.

· Do lots of exam questions. Chemistry questions often apply familiar equations/concepts in different situations.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in Chemistry!

· Get comfortable with multi-step calculations and rearranging equations.

· Learn how to explain patterns using particle or atomic structure rather than just describing them.

· Practise unfamiliar application questions. The information you need is sometimes hidden in the question.

· Be precise with scientific terminology. "It reacts" is very different from explaining how and why it reacts.

· For calculations, always check units, significant figures and whether your answer is sensible.

· Do mixed topic papers reguarly.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE Chemistry. These topics are commonly tested in the exams and may get you a few easy marks.

· The differences between atoms, elements, compounds and mixtures.

· Atomic structure, including protons, neutrons, electrons and isotopes.

· The structure of the Periodic Table and how elements are arranged.

· Electronic structure and how it relates to chemical reactions.

· Ionic, covalent and metallic bonding and their properties.

· How to write and balance chemical equations.

· The difference between acids, alkalis and neutral substances, including the pH scale.

· Reactivity of metals and the reactions of metals with oxygen, water and acids.

· Acids, bases, neutralisation and making salts.

· Electrolysis and how to identify the products formed.

· The factors affecting the rate of a chemical reaction.

· Reversible reactions and dynamic equilibrium.

· Exothermic and endothermic reactions and energy changes.

· Crude oil, hydrocarbons and fractional distillation.

· Alkanes, alkenes and the reactions of hydrocarbons.

· The Earth's atmosphere, greenhouse gases and climate change.

· The composition of the atmosphere and how it has changed over time.

· The properties and uses of metals, polymers and other materials.

· Tests for common gases and ions, including the expected observations.

· Required practicals and how to analyse and evaluate experimental results.
