Top Tips:

· Learn developmental milestones in order. It's much easier to remember what happens when you understand how development progresses.

· Revise the four areas of development separately: physical, intellectual/cognitive, emotional, social.

· Learn examples of how children develop, not just definitions.

· Understand how nutrition affects development and health.

· Know the difference between growth and development.

· Learn about factors that can affect development, such as environment, relationships, nutrition and health.

· For scenario questions, apply your knowledge to the child in the question rather than giving a generic answer.

· Practise extended questions so you can explain why something matters.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in Childcare!

· Always explain the consequence, such as "A balanced diet provides the nutrients required for healthy growth and development." Then apply it to the situation in the question.

· Make connections.

· Apply everything. If the question gives you a scenario, use the information provided.

· Consider individual differences. Children don't all develop at exactly the same rate. When appropriate, consider other factors such as health or environment.

· For calculations, always check units, significant figures and whether your answer is sensible.

· Do mixed topic papers reguarly.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE Childcare and Child Development. The exact content will depend on your qualification and exam board. This is just to be used as a guide to help you revise.

· The difference between growth and development.

· The four main areas of development: physical, intellectual, emotional and social.

· Typical developmental milestones from birth through early childhood.

· Factors that can influence a child's growth and development.

· The importance of play in children's development.

· Different types of play and the skills they can help children develop.

· The importance of communication and interaction for children's development.

· The importance of attachment and positive relationships.

· The nutritional needs of babies, children and pregnant women.

· The importance of a balanced diet for growth and development.

· The main nutrients and their functions in the body.

· The importance of hydration and healthy eating habits.

· Breastfeeding, formula feeding and the introduction of solid foods where relevant to your course.

· Common childhood illnesses, their symptoms and methods of prevention.

· Immunisation and its importance in preventing infectious diseases.

· Hygiene practices that help protect babies and children from infection.

· Safety risks in the home and childcare environment.

· Ways of preventing accidents and reducing risks to children.

· The importance of safeguarding children and recognising signs that may indicate a concern.

· The role of parents, carers and childcare professionals.

· The importance of providing a safe, stimulating and inclusive environment.

· The effects of different environments and experiences on children's development.

· The importance of meeting individual children's physical, intellectual, emotional and social needs.

· Relevant legislation, guidance and professional responsibilities for childcare.

· How to apply knowledge to childcare scenarios and explain the reasons for recommended actions.

· How to use accurate childcare terminology in extended answers.
