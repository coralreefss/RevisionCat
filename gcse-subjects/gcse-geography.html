Top Tips:

· Learn case studies properly. Know the place, key facts, statistics and specific examples.

· Don't just memorise facts, instead understand why something happens and what its impacts are.

· Use diagram and be able to explain them.

· Learn precise geographical terminology.

· Practise map skills: scales, grid references, directions, contours and interpreting maps.

· Practise data questions such as graphs.

· Practise 6 and 9 mark questions under timed conditions.

· Always answer the actual question. Don't just dump everything you know about the topic.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in Geography!

· Use specific statistics and named examples rather than vague statements.

· For evaluation questions, make your judgement clear and justified.

· Compare factors rather than discussing them completely separately.

· Learn alternative viewpoints for issues such as climate change, development and resource management.

· Use geographical terminology naturally rather than forcing it into every sentence.

· For physical Geography, explain the process and the resulting landform/hazard.

· For human Geography, connect social, economic, political and environmental factors.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE Geography. The specific topics, case studies and examples you need to learn will depend on your exam board and chosen course.

· The key physical and human Geography topics included in your specification.

· The location and key characteristics of the countries, regions and places studied.

· Important case studies and specific examples required by your course.

· Key statistics and facts that can be used as evidence in exam answers.

· The causes, effects and responses to tectonic hazards.

· The causes, effects and management of weather hazards and extreme weather.

· Climate change, its causes, effects and possible responses.

· The processes of erosion, transportation and deposition.

· The formation of important river and coastal landforms.

· River and coastal management strategies and their advantages and disadvantages.

· Ecosystems, including food chains, nutrient cycles and the interaction between living and non-living components.

· Tropical rainforests and their importance, threats and management.

· Hot deserts and the challenges of living in and managing desert environments.

· Population distribution, population change and factors affecting population.

· Migration and its causes and impacts.

· Urbanisation and the growth of cities.

· The opportunities and challenges created by urban growth.

· Development, including measures of development and differences between countries.

· Globalisation and the factors that influence the global economy.

· Resource management, including the supply and demand of important resources.

· Sustainable development and ways of managing geographical challenges.

· Map skills, including scale, grid references, direction, contours and symbols.

· How to interpret photographs, maps, graphs, charts and geographical data.

· How to calculate and interpret common geographical data and statistics.

· Fieldwork methods, including data collection, sampling and presenting results.

· How to evaluate the reliability and limitations of geographical data.

· How to use evidence and examples to support an argument.

· How to structure extended answers and reach a clear, justified conclusion.
