Top Tips:

· Learn key religious teachings and what they mean. Don't just memorise quotations.

· Know different viewpoints. RS questions often reward understanding that members of the same religion may disagree.

· Learn key terminology precisely.

· Practise applying beliefs to ethical issues.

· Don't forget non-religious viewpoints if your specification includes them.

· Practise 4/5-mark questions as well as the longer ones.

· Use examples. Real-world examples can make an argument much stronger when relevant.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in Religious Studies.

· Don't just name a teaching, explain it. For example, don't just say "The Bible teaches that you should love your neighbour." Explain what this means and how it applies to the question.

· Compare viewpoints properly, such as "Both viewpoints agree that X is important, but they differ because..."

· Evaluate the argument. Don't just describe a viewpoint, explain why it is strong or weak and how it compares to other viewpoints.

· Learn a small number of quotations very well.

· Understand different interpretations.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE Religious Studies. The specific religions, beliefs and ethical topics you need to learn will depend on your exam board and chosen course.

· The key beliefs and teachings of the religions studied on your course.

· Important religious practices, festivals, rituals and forms of worship.

· Key religious terms and their meanings.

· Important religious teachings and quotations from sacred texts.

· Different interpretations of religious teachings.

· Differences between denominations or groups where relevant.

· Religious arguments for and against important ethical issues.

· Non-religious viewpoints, including atheist or humanist perspectives, where relevant to your course.

· Arguments supporting and opposing different ethical positions.

· The difference between a religious belief, teaching, practice and ethical argument.

· How religious beliefs can influence people's actions and decisions.

· How to apply religious teachings to modern ethical issues.

· How to use quotations and teachings as evidence in an answer.

· How to compare different religious and non-religious viewpoints.

· How to explain an argument rather than simply stating it.

· How to develop a chain of reasoning throughout an answer.

· How to evaluate arguments using strengths, weaknesses and counterarguments.

· How to reach a clear and justified conclusion in evaluation questions.

· How to use key religious terminology accurately.

· How to respond to different question types and command words.
