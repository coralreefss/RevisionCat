Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for GCSE Maths. These are the resources I found most useful when revising for my exams. However, these are just my recommendations and opinions based off of what I found most useful. You may find other resources more useful for your revision!

1. CorbettMaths

CorbettMaths was one of my personal favourite websites for GCSE Maths. They have videos per topic, worksheets, and cover general revision. I did the 5-a-day worksheet every day for months leading up to my exam and I feel it really helped as it forced me to revisit older, previous topics. They also offer further resources that you can buy if wanted.

2. Maths Genie

Maths Genie was a very helpful resource too. The website provides topic-by-topic videos, notes, practice questions and exam papers. It's especially good for working through a topic and then immediately practising it.

3. Physics and Maths Tutor (PMT)

PMT provides a huge collection of GCSE Maths past papers, topic questions and mark schemes. They are especially useful for exam/question practice.

YouTube Channels:

If you're looking for some Youtube channels to help you revise, below are a few I found useful when revising for my GCSE Maths.

1. Maths Genie

Maths Genie provides many videos explaining indiviual topics as well as full paper walkthroughs. The videos are mainly targeted towards Edexcel, but most topics may apply to similar exam boards. All videos are sections into playlists and clearly separated into higher and foundation.

2. CorbettMaths

CorbettMaths is another channel you may find useful for revising Maths. There are a wide range of videos from individual topics to 5-a-day video solutions, as well as full paper walkthroughs. They also cover multiple exam boards at GCSE and Alevel. CorbettMaths is one of my favourites due to the easy to understand videos.

3. HannahKettleMaths

HannahKettleMaths is another channel you may find useful. She is a real GCSE Maths examiner and covers individual topics to "half paper revision" videos. She also provides a "best guess" paper every year. Personally, I found this very useful, especially if you have run out of past papers to do. She also provides FREE live revision sessions every Thursday, 7-7:30pm for Foundation and 7:30-8pm for Higher.

Top Tips:

· Don't just watch videos. Do questions immediately afterwards to ensure you understand.

· Show all your working. Even if you can do something mentally, writing it down can earn method marks.

· Keep a mistakes list. Write down the topics/questions you repeatedly get wrong and revisit them.

· Practise under timed conditions. Being able to solve something isn't enough if you can't finish the paper.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in Maths!

· Don't avoid the horrible questions. The questions you're least comfortable with are often where the biggest improvements happen.

· Practise multi-step problems rather than only individual topic questions.

· Get comfortable with proof, algebraic reasoning and problem-solving.

· When you get a question wrong, don't just write the correct answer. Work out where your method went wrong.

· Do mixed-topic papers regularly.

· Practise questions where you have to figure out the method yourself rather than simply applying a formula you've memorised.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE Maths. These topics are commonly tested in the exams and may get you a few easy marks.

· Fractions, decimals, percentages and percentage change.

· Ratio, proportion and direct/inverse proportion.

· Indices, standard form and surds.

· Algebraic manipulation, including factorising and completing the square.

· Solving linear, quadratic and simultaneous equations.

· Sequences and nth term.

· Pythagoras and trigonometry.

· Exact values and trigonometric graphs.

· Circle theorems.

· Bounds and error intervals.

· Circle theorems.

· Graphs, gradients and equations of lines.

· Probability, including tree diagrams and conditional probability.
