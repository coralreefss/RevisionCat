Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for GCSE Business revision. These are the resources I found most useful when revising for my exams. However, these are just my recommendations and opinions based off of what I found most useful. You may find other resources more useful for your revision!

1. LitCharts

LitCharts was one of my personal favourite websites for GCSE English Lit. They cover almost 3500 titles, each with detailed descriptions, analysis and summaries. They also have information on characters, quotes and themes. As well as plays and texts, LitCharts also covers poetry and literary terms. If I could strongly recommend one website, it would be this one.

2. KnowUnity

KnowUnity During my GCSEs, I also used KnowUnity quite a lot for notes or summaries. Notes and resources are provided by other students or teachers. You could also publish your own resources too! I found KnowUnity very helpful for plot summaries, character details, and deeper analysis.

3. BBC Bitesize

BBC Bitesize provides notes, video explanations and quizzes to help you revise and understand content. There is also a section on exam technique and how to use past papers for each exam board! There are also many podcasts and a guide to writing poetry analysis.

YouTube Channels:

If you're looking for some Youtube channels to help you revise, below are a few I found useful when revising for my GCSE English Literature exams.

1. Mr Bruff

Mr Bruff provides text analysis and exam technique. His exam technique is tailored to AQA, but the analysis of the texts and plays will apply to any exam board.

2. Mr Salles Teaches English

Mr Salles Teaches English is another channel you may find useful for revising English Lit. He covers a range of exam boards and poetry anthologies. He explains the content and provides analysis. His channel is geared towards students aiming for grades 7,8 and 9, but the content can apply to any grade if you are willing to put in the effort.

3. Mr Everything English

Mr Everything English is another channel you may find useful. He covers many of the plays, such as "Macbeth" and "Jekyll and Hyde". Most of his videos are relatively short, but he also covers full papers.

4. The Lightup Hub

The Lightup Hub is a channel I did not use as much as the others, but I still highly recommend it. She provides high grade analysis and paragraph structures. The YouTube channel is free, but you have to pay for the content on the website. She also covers some of the plays and texts.

Top Tips:

· Know your texts really well. Don't just memorise plot summaries.

· Memorise short "juicy" quotations you can say a lot about, rather than huge chunks of dialogue.

· Zoom in on individual words. Ask why the writer chose that particularword and what effect it has.

· Always consider language AND structure.

· Make sure every quotation actually proves or adds to your point.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in English Literature!

· Analyse, don't just identify. Instead of: "The writer uses a metaphor." Go further: What does the metaphor suggest? Why that metaphor? What does it reveal about the character or theme? How might the audience respond?

· Have multiple interpretations. This earns the top marks.

· Talk about the writer's intentions. You could use the structure: Writer's choice, meaning, bigger idea, audience effect.

· Make connections across the text. This could tie into character development. For example, you could reference Lady Macbeth's slow decent into insanity due to guilt, that ultimately lead to her downfall.

· Use context naturally. Don't try to force it where it doesn't fit or doesn't make sense.

· Don't force fancy vocabulary. Precise analysis beats throwing complicated words into every sentence.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE English Literature. These topics are commonly tested in the exams and may get you a few easy marks.

· Plot and structure of each set text.

· Main characters and how they develop.

· Major themes and how they change throughout the text.

· Key quotations for each character/theme (I recommend learning maximum 20 quotations per text. Many quotes can relate to multiple themes or characters).

· Important language and structure techniques.

· The writer's intentions and messages.

· Different possible interpretations of important moments.

· How characters and themes contrast with one another.

· How the beginning, middle and ending of the text connect.

· How to integrate quotations into your own sentences.

· How to compare poems through methods, meaning and context.
