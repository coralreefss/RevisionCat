Top Tips:

· Always read the question twice before answering.

· Use quotations to support every analysis point.

· Learn a wide range of language and structural techniques: how to spot them and use them in your own writing (e.g. personification or cyclical structure).

· Practise writing under timed conditions.

· Plan your creative writing before you begin.

· Always explain the effect on the reader, not just identify the technique.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in English Language!

· Zoom in on individual words and explain why the writer chose them. (e.g. "Her red hair flew in the wind": why did the writer choose "flew" instead of "moved"? What effect does this have on the reader? What does the colour red connote? What could that suggest about the story or character?)

· Use varied sentence structures and ambitious vocabulary in creative writing.

· Link your points together instead of writing isolated paragraphs.

· Practise with unseen extracts regularly—don't only revise familiar ones.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE English Language. These topics are commonly tested in the exams and may get you a few easy marks.

· The differences between language techniques and structural techniques.

· The purpose and effect of similes, metaphors, personification, pathetic fallacy and symbolism.

· Structural devices such as foreshadowing, flashbacks, cyclical structure and shifts in focus.

· How to identify tone, mood and atmosphere.

· How to compare two texts effectively.

· How to write persuasive, descriptive and narrative pieces.

· Punctuation for effect (colons, semicolons, dashes, ellipsis and varied sentence lengths).

· How to plan and structure an extended response.

· Common exam command words (analyse, compare, evaluate, explain and summarise).

· How to evaluate effectively.

· How to use evidence effectively in your answers.
