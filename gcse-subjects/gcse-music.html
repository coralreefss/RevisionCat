Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for GCSE Music revision. These are the resources I found through research as I did not take Music myself. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. BBC Bitesize

BBC Bitesize provides notes, video explanations and quizzes to help you revise and understand content.

2. Focus on Sound

Focus on Sound provides interactive activities, instrument recognition and listening exercises. Many schools provide free access.

3. Music Matters (YouTube)

Music Matters provides engaging video content that covers various aspects of music theory and practice. The channel is great for visual learners and those who prefer a more interactive approach to learning.

Top Tips:

· Listen to your set works regularly—not just when revising

· Learn the key musical vocabulary and use it confidently in written answers.

· Practise identifying instruments by their sound.

· When listening to music, think about melody, harmony, rhythm, tempo, dynamics and texture.

· Revise little and often. Music theory is much easier to remember through regular practice.

· If your course includes performance, practise consistently rather than leaving it until the deadline.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 (equivalent of A or A*) in Music!

· Use precise musical terminology instead of vague descriptions.

· Link every musical feature to its effect on the listener.

· Learn the characteristics of each musical period (Baroque, Classical, Romantic, Modern).

· In listening questions, identify multiple musical elements, not just one.

· If composing, develop your musical ideas rather than repeating the same motif.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE Music. These topics are commonly tested in the exams and may get you a few easy marks.

· The elements of music (melody, harmony, rhythm, tempo, dynamics, texture, timbre, structure and tonality).

· The differences between the Baroque, Classical, Romantic and 20th/21st Century musical periods.

· Common musical instruments and their families (strings, brass, woodwind, percussion and keyboard).

· Musical vocabulary such as crescendo, diminuendo, staccato, legato, pizzicato, syncopation and ostinato.

· Major vs minor keys and basic chords.

· THow to recognise different textures (monophonic, homophonic and polyphonic).

· Your set works (composer, context, musical features and important sections).

· How to identify musical elements in listening questions.

· Basic notation, note values, rests and time signatures.

· Common musical forms (binary, ternary, rondo, verse-chorus and theme & variations).
