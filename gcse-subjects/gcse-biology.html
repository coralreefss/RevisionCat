Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for GCSE Biology revision. These are the resources I found most useful when revising for my exams. However, these are just my recommendations and opinions based off of what I found most useful. You may find other resources more useful for your revision!

1. Cognito

Cognito is my personal favourite website for GCSE Biology (and science in general). It has (short) animated lessons and quizzes to help you learn and understand content. You can access this through Youtube or Cognito's Website.

2. Physics and Maths Tutor (PMT)

PMT provides comprehensive notes, practice questions and video tutorials to help you understand the concepts. I also found this useful as everything was in one place.

3. BBC Bitesize

BBC Bitesize provides notes, video explanations and quizzes to help you revise and understand content. There are also written explanations and diagrams of practicals!

YouTube Channels:

For me, I mainly revised science through Youtube videos and flashcards. If you're looking for some Youtube channels to help you revise, below are a few I found useful when revising for my GCSE Biology exams.

1. Cognito

Cognito , as mentioned above, is one of my favourite channels for revising science. Their videos are relatively short and easy to understand. Cognito helped me to understand tricky topics and also colour code everything!

2. Freesciencelessons

Free Science Lessons is another channel I found useful for revising science. They have a range of videos covering all topics in the specification. The videos are easy to follow and understand. However, I did find some of them quite boring.

3. Bryn Irons

Bryn Irons is another channel I LOVED for science. The videos are created by a med student. I found his channel particularly useful as he moved quite quickly through the content and explained things simply and clearly. I found his "entire paper 1/2" videos particularly useful.

4. ScienceShorts

ScienceShorts is a channel I did not use as much as the others, but I still highly recommend it. Like Cognito, the videos are animated, short and easy to understand.

5. Science with Hazel

Science with Hazel has many longer videos that cover a whole paper in one sitting. She covers GCSE, IGCSE, and Alevel content, as well as a variety of exam boards, so make sure you select the right video for your exam board!

Top Tips:

· Try to (roughly) draw diagrams from memory (e.g. the heart, the eye, etc..).

· Learn required practicals early: they come up every single year.

· Learn scientific vocabulary carefully. One incorrect word or incorrect terminology can lose marks.

· Practice interpreting and drawing graphs. This a common question in the exam, and is usually worth 3-4 marks.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in Biology!

· Memorise command words (describe, state, explain). These often define how you should answer a question. For example, if a question asks you to "describe" something, you should not bjust state a fact, but instead describe the process or concept in detail.

· Learn mark scheme phrases. Examiners look for specific scientific wording.

· Practice 6 markers regularly.

· Understand why processes happen, not just what happens. This makes unfamiliar questions much easier.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE Biology. These topics are commonly tested in the exams and may get you a few easy marks.

· The differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

· The functions of the cell organelles (nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, chloroplasts, vacuole, cell membrane, cell wall).

· The differences between diffusion, osmosis and active transport.

· Factors that affect enzyme activity and why enzymes denature.

· The differences between mitosis and meiosis.

· The structure of the heart and the path blood takes through the body.

· How vaccines, antibiotics and the immune system work.

· The structure of the digestive system and how enzymes digest food

· How to complete a Punnett square and calculate genetic probabilities.

· The carbon cycle and water cycle.

· Required practicals and how to analyse results (usually a graph)
