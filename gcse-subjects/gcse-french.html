Top Tips:

· Learn vocabulary in chunks, not isolated words. For example, learning a useful phrase is often more helpful than memorising one noun.

· Learn verbs very well. Including their conjugations and how to use them in different tenses.

· Know your opinion phrases and connectives.

· Practise all four skills: reading, writing, listening and speaking.

· Listen to French regularly. Even 5 to 10 minutes helps you get used to the sounds.

· Don't translate English word-for-word. French often structures sentences differently.

·For speaking: prepare flexible phrases rather than memorising an entire script.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in French!

· Use a range of tenses. Show off!

· Use sophisticated connectives, such as "cependant" (however), "pourtant" (however), and "en revanche" (on the other hand).

· Learn some flexible idioms, such as "avoir le cafard" (to feel down).

· Use the subjunctive, such as "Il faut que je fasse..." (it is necessary that I do...).

· Learn adjectives and their agreements. Remember that adjectives can change according to gender and amount.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE French. These topics are commonly tested in the exams and may get you a few easy marks.

· Common French vocabulary for family, friends, relationships and descriptions.

· Vocabulary for school, education, subjects, teachers and future plans.

· Vocabulary for hobbies, sports, free time, music, films and television.

· Vocabulary for holidays, travel, accommodation and transport.

· Vocabulary for food, restaurants, shopping and healthy living.

· Vocabulary for jobs, careers, money and ambitions.

· Vocabulary for the environment, climate change and protecting the planet.

· Vocabulary for technology, social media and the internet.

· Vocabulary for society, communities, charities and social issues.

· High-frequency verbs, including être, avoir, aller, faire, pouvoir, vouloir and devoir.

· The present tense and how to form regular and common irregular verbs.

· The perfect tense, including avoir and être as auxiliary verbs.

· The imperfect tense and when it is used.

· The near future and simple future tenses.

· The conditional tense and how to express what you would do.

· The difference between masculine and feminine nouns.

· Adjective agreement and the position of common adjectives.

· Articles, including un, une, des, le, la, l' and les.

· Negatives, including ne...pas and other negative structures.

· Question words and how to form different types of questions.

· Object pronouns and common pronoun structures.

· Comparatives and superlatives.

· Common irregular verbs.

· How to express opinions, reasons, advantages, disadvantages and consequences.

· Common idioms and phrases that can be adapted to different questions.

· How to identify key information and distractors in listening and reading questions.
