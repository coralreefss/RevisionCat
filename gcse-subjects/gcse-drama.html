Top Tips:

· Learn your drama terminology—using technical vocabulary earns marks.

· When analysing acting, always explain how a technique communicates meaning.

· Revise using videos of live performances, not just scripts.

· Know your set text inside out—quotes, stage directions and key moments.

· Always justify your ideas. Don't just say "I'd use lighting". Explain why.

· Put energy and emotion into your acting. Don't just say "I'd be sad". Show it through your performance.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in Drama!

· Use precise theatrical vocabulary naturally throughout your answers.

· Link every acting or design choice back to the audience's response.

· Compare different staging ideas rather than describing just one.

· Think like a director. Explain why every movement, lighting cue, costume or sound effect has been chosen and what it conveys.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE Drama. These topics are commonly tested in the exams and may get you a few easy marks.

· The differences between physical, vocal and facial acting skills.

· Key stage positions (upstage, downstage, stage left, stage right, centre stage).

· Theatre terminology (blocking, proxemics, levels, pace, pause, gesture, mime, tableau).

· The purpose of lighting, sound, costume, set and props.

· Your set text (characters, themes, key scenes and important quotations).

· Your live theatre evaluation (acting, design and audience impact).

· How to explain the effect on the audience.

· Different performance styles (e.g. naturalism, physical theatre, Brecht, Frantic Assembly, etc... depending on your course).

· The roles of theatre professionals (director, designer, lighting designer, stage manager, costume designer).

· How to structure extended exam answers using evidence and drama terminology.
