Top Tips:

· Learn the key definitions. They're worth easy marks.

· Don't just memorise body systems, understand how they work during exercise.

· Practise interpreting graphs and fitness test results.

· Revise using diagrams of the heart, lungs and muscles.

· Learn sporting examples for every topic. You'll need them in longer questions.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in PE!

· Use accurate scientific vocabulary throughout your answers.

· Always apply your knowledge to the sport mentioned in the question.

· Practice 6 markers regularly.

· Explain why, not just what.

· Memorise all required fitness tests and what they measure.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE PE. These topics are commonly tested in the exams and may get you a few easy marks.

· The components of fitness (speed, strength, power, flexibility, balance, coordination, agility, reaction time, cardiovascular endurance and muscular endurance).

· The differences between the aerobic and anaerobic energy systems.

· The structure and function of the heart, lungs and circulatory system.

· The major muscles and bones in the body.

· The different types of movement (flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation and circumduction).

· The principles of training (SPORT & FITT).

· Methods of training (continuous, interval, fartlek, circuit, weight and plyometric training).

· Fitness testing (purpose, procedure and advantages/disadvantages of each test).

· The effects of exercise on the body (heart rate, breathing rate, stroke volume and recovery).

· Sports psychology (motivation, guidance, feedback, arousal and goal setting).

· Socio-cultural influences (participation, barriers, sponsorship, media and commercialisation).
