Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for GCSE History. These are the resources I found through research as I did not take History myself. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. BBC Bitesize

BBC Bitesize is a very useful resource for GCSE History. As mentioned above, it provides podcasts and videos for lots of historical topics. It also has quizzes and questions to test your knowledge.

2. Physics and Maths Tutor (PMT)

PMT was a very helpful resource too. Here is a bank of past papers and mark schemes. Particularly useful once you've learnt the content.

3. KnowUnity

KnowUnity can be very useful for notes or diagrams provided by teachers or other students. Just ensure that you check the information is correct and relevant to your exam board.

YouTube Channels:

If you're looking for some Youtube channels to help you revise, below are a few I found through research for GCSE History.

1. History with Hilbert

History with Hilbert provides a wide variety of videos on different historical topics and events. The videos are clear and concise, and quite short.

2. Simple History

Simple History is another channel you may find useful for revising History. They also provide animated videos on a wide range of historical topics. This channel is great for getting the "big picture" of historical events.

3. Oversimplified

Oversimplified is another channel you may find useful. They are not GCSE-specific, but they provide entertaining and informative videos on historical events. It is great for making major historical events more memorable and easier to understand.

4. Horrible Histories

Horrible Histories is another channel you may find useful. While it is a children's channel and TV show, it provides entertaining and informative content on historical events. Plus, the songs could help with remembering facts!

Top Tips:

· Make timelines. History becomes much easier when you know what happened before and after something. You can also find timetables on KnowUnity.

· Learn causes and consequences. Don't just memorise what happened.

· Know turning points. Understand why an event was significant.

· Learn specific evidence. Names, dates, statistics, laws, events and examples can strengthen answers.

· Don't learn every date ever. Prioritise dates that help establish chronology or are particularly significant.

· Do timed essays. Knowing the history is useless if you can't get it onto the page in the time.

· Build chains of reasoning.

Grade 8/9 Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve a grade 8 or 9 ( equivalent of A or A*) in History.

· Be precise with evidence

· Build developed explanations.

· Make comparisons. For questions asking about factors, don't treat each factor like a completely separate mini-essay. Compare them: "Factor A was important because..., however Factor B had a greater impact because..."

· Understand interpretations.

· Analyse sources rather than describing them

· For Ancient History, know the society as well as the events. If your Ancient topic involves a civilisation such as Rome, Greece or Egypt, don't only learn battles and rulers. Also learn about the society, culture, religion and everyday life.

Must-Know Information:

Below, you can find a small checklist of must-know information for GCSE History. The specific events, people and periods you need to learn will depend on your exam board and chosen topics.

· The chronological order of the main events in each of your History topics.

· The key people, groups and organisations involved in each historical period.

· The main causes of important historical events.

· The short-term and long-term consequences of important events.

· The significance of major events and turning points.

· Important dates, laws, treaties, battles and political developments.

· The political, social, economic and religious factors affecting each period.

· The beliefs, attitudes and experiences of different groups within society.

· Change and continuity over time.

· Similarities and differences between people, events and historical periods.

· Key terminology specific to each historical topic.

· How to use specific historical evidence to support an argument.

· How to explain the importance of a historical factor rather than simply describing it.

· How to compare different factors and reach a supported judgement.

· How to analyse the content and provenance of historical sources.

· How to explain why a source may be useful or limited.

· How to identify and analyse different historical interpretations.

· How to support or challenge an interpretation using your own historical knowledge.

· How to structure extended History answers using clear points, evidence and explanations.

· How to reach a clear, justified judgement in questions that require evaluation.
