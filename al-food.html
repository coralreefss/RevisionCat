Top Tips:

· Learn the science behind food, not just the facts. Understand why proteins denature, why starch gelatinises, why emulsions work, what heat does to nutrients, etc.

· Understand food safety as a process. Know hazards, risks, contamination, microorganisms, temperature control, hygiene and prevention rather than memorising isolated definitions.

· Learn food science through experiments.

· Practise interpreting results.

· Make an error log. Record recurring mistakes rather than simply recording your percentage.

· Keep a food-science vocabulary bank. Terms such as denaturation, gelatinisation, dextrinisation, etc... may need to become automatic.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* in Food Science and Nutrition!

· Memorise command words (describe, state, explain). These often define how you should answer a question. For example, if a question asks you to "describe" something, you should not just state a fact, but instead describe the process or concept in detail.

· Answer the question, not the topic. If the question asks why a particular result occurred, don't dump everything you know about that biological process.

· Use examiner comments. They can reveal exactly where students commonly lose marks.

· Justify every recommendation. Instead of "use this cooking method", explain why it is appropriate for the food, target consumer, nutritional requirements and desired result.

· Use scientific terminology accurately. High-level answers need precision.

· Think about confounding variables. If you're investigating one factor, consider what else could have affected the result.

· Practise writing high-scoring answers under time pressure. At A* level, knowing the information isn't enough if you can't communicate it quickly and precisely.
