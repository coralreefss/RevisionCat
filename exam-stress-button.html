Managing Exam Stress

Exams can be stressful. Many exams and high expectations from others can be a lot. However, this stress is normal and can be managed! What is stress?

Stress is, according to Google, the body's natural physical and mental response to any demand, challenge or change. It is a biological reaction that prepares you to react to threats: the fight, flight or freeze response.

Although stress is a natural response designed to keep us safe, it can become overwhelming when it happens too often or lasts for a long period of time. Many students experience stress during exams due to: pressure to achieve, large amounts of revision, fear of failure or worries about the future. A small amount of stress can actually be helpful, as it can improve focus and motivation, but too much stress can negatively affect both your mental and physical wellbeing. Personally, I think understanding stress is the first step to overcoming it!

Symptoms of Stress

Symptoms of stress can vary between people. Some may experience stress more severely, while others may not. These symptoms could be expressed physically, mentally and emotionally, or behaviourally. Some common symptoms are listed below for reference. If your symptom/s specifically aren't listed, that doesn't mean what you are feeling is invalid. It could just be how stress is presented in you personally. However, if you are concerned about your health, it is always best to visit a doctor or GP!

Physical Symptoms of Stress:

· Headaches or dizziness,

· Tight muscles or body pain,

· Fast heart rate,

· Chest pain,

· Upset stomach, nausea, or digestion issues,

· Feeling very tired, even if sleeping well.

Mental and Emotional Symptoms of Stress:

· Feeling anxious or nervous,

· Trouble focusing or remembering things,

· Feeling sad,

· Being quick to anger or easily annoyed (irritable),

· Constant worrying,

· Overthinking.

Behavioural Symptoms of Stress:

· Sleeping too much or too little,

· Eating more or less than usual,

· Loss of appetite,

· Using alcohol, smoking, or taking drugs more often,

· Stopping things you usually enjoy.

Quick Calm Down Techniques

There are many techniques you can use to try to calm yourself when you are feeling stressed. For long term stress relief, you could also try journaling, scrapbooking, or taking up a new hobby such as sewing, knitting, drawing, colouring, a sport... anything! A few short term solutions are listed below:

Box Breathing:

· 4 seconds in, 4 seconds hold, 4 seconds out, 4 seconds hold, and repeat.

Go For a Walk:

· Even a 5 minute walk can help to clear your mind and help you relax! As a bonus, try to find 5 things you can see, 4 things you can touch, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, 1 thing you can taste! This technique is often used to stop racing thoughts and "ground" you to the present!

Listen to Music

· Listening to music can help to focus you. Slower music, particularly around 60 beats per minute, helps with relaxation and mediation. Instumental music can be beneficial if you are about to revise or work on a task that requires your attention. Drink Some Water:

· It sounds obvious but dehydration can genuinely make concentration worse. The NHS recommends 6 to 8 cups or 1.5 to 2 litres of water, milk or sugar-free drinks like tea or coffee per day.

Night Before an Exam

The night before an exam, especially the first one, can be very stressful. You may feel the need to stay up all night, desperately relearning the spec or watching videos. This is known as "cramming". Cramming is studying intensively to absorb a large volume of information in a short period of time.

This often fails long term because it focuses on memorising facts quickly rather than building a deeper understanding. It can often lead to sleep loss and exhaustion, which is not ideal before an exam. There are more effective ways to revise. These can be found in the How To Revise section (the question mark icon at the top!)

In all honesty, you should stop heavy revising early. I suggest around 7pm as a cutoff time and try to be asleep around 10pm to 12am. An extra hour of sleep will benefit you more compared to an hour of intense cramming. Instead, you should:

GET ORGANISED:

· Pack your pencil case (3 black pens, a pencil, rubber, ruler, protractor, compass, and a highlighter or two).

· Lay out your uniform or clothes for the next day,

· Check the location of your exam. Ensure you know the room number (usually the sports hall), time, and travel route,

· Set two (or more) alarms and place your phone across the room so you have to get up.

WIND DOWN AND REST:

· Stop all studying at least an hour or two before you go top bed to let your mind relax. Sleep is so important before an exam.

· Eat a healthy and filling dinner.

Turn off bright screens and lights early to help yourself fall asleep faster.

Morning Before an Exam

The morning before an exam can be just as daunting as the night before an exam. Here are some last minute tips if you're feeling nervous!

Light Revision:

You can go over flashcards or mindmaps (or other resources) before you leave for your exam. Similarly, you could watch recap videos on weaker topics or watch past paper walkthroughs. However, if you do revise, ensure it is not strenuous so you don't burn yourself out before the exam itself!

Check Equipment:

Self explanatory. Do a quick check and make sure you have all the equipment you may need. Such as a calculator or more than one pen.

Have a healthy breakfast:

The exam will be much harder if you're taking it on an empty stomach. Even if you're nervous, try to manage some toast or ceral. Fruit has also been proven to stimulate your brain!

During Revision Tips

Revision can be difficult, but it doesn't have to be! Here are some small tips for while you're revising. You can find more tips as well as common revision techniques in the How To Revise section (or by clicking the black question mark icon in the top right!)

· Try doing 20 to 30 minute sessions,

· Take regular breaks,

· Don't compare yourself to your friends! Everyone revises and learns differently!

· Revise topics you struggle with (not just the ones you already know and understand!)

· Start with something you know if you're stuck or are lacking motivation.

During Exam Tips

During an exam, especially the first one, it can be easy to panic and freeze. The pressure of the past few months and the reality of it can be daunting. Below, you can find some tips and advice on what to do and how to avoid stress when you're in an exam.

Read Every Question Carefully:

· Read each question twice. Make sure you fully understand what the question is asking of you before you start to analyse it. Command words such as 'state' and 'explain' have different meanings and require different answers. This is also addressed in the How To Revise section!

Underline/highlight the command words:

· Once you understand the question and have identified the command words, you may find it helpful to highlight it or make it stand out, to ensure you answer the question correctly.

If you don't know, move on!

· If you are unsure of a question, even if it is multiple choice, circle the question number and move on. If you have time, come back to it later. Do not waste lots of time on a low mark question.

Keep an eye on the time:

· For many students, time management is often the biggest difficulty during the exams. They run out of time and are then unable to answer every question. It may help you to timestamp occassionally. I found it especially useful in English exams, when time management is key. I would write down the start and end time per question, and aim for a minute a mark. I recommend this method if you think it may help. Exam halls usually have both an analogue and digital clock, so even if you cannot read an analogue clock or are sat near the back, you can still be aware of the time.

Try to answer every question:

· Every mark counts. Try to answer every question and leave nothing blank, if possible. Even if you think you're wrong, write an answer. You may think it's dumb, but it could be accepted on the mark scheme - especially if many other students say similar things.

If You're Feeling Overwhelmed...

It is normal to feel overwhelmed during exams, especially if they are mocks, GCSEs or Alevels. It can take a lot of time, effort and work, and sometimes it can feel like too much. If you start to feel overwhelmed, remove yourself from the area and take a walk, or listen to music, to help clear your mind. You may find it helpful to look at the "Quick Calm Down Techniques". You can access this quickly by clicking the cloud icon at the top, and then finding the quick link to the Calming strategies. Just remember, you're not alone, and you've got this!

FAQs!

What if I go blank?

That's okay, and it's normal to go blank under pressure! You should take a breath and try to focus. Try to attempt the question. If you're really unsure, circle the question number and come back to the question later on if you have time.

What if I fail?

Failing is not ideal, but it is not the end of the world. There are measures in place to support those who do not manage to achieve passing grades. If you do not achieve a Grade 4 or above, you will need to retake Maths and English Language. There are opportunities to resit these two exams in November. Additionally, some colleges and further education teach GCSE Maths and English for those who need to retake it. While it can be upsetting, you will be okay and there are still things you can do if you do not get a Grade 4 or above.

How much revision is enough?

The amount of time every person will need / want to revise for is different and can vary massively. Some students will not revise at all, and still do very well. Others may do an hour or two a day. Others may do more. Revision is more about finding what works for you. Do you prefer revising in large chunks in one day, or do you prefer doing one hour every day of the week? It is a bit of trial-and-error, but you will find what works for you! However, do not feel like you have to consistently do 9 or 10 hour study days. You will burn yourself out rather quickly. Personally, I did about 1 hour a day in February, and then 3 to 5 hours of revision from March to the end of exams (June).

Should I revise the night before?

You can revise the night before if you believe it will help you during the exam. Some last minute revision could be active recall or flashcards. In my opinion, past papers are one of the best last minute revision methods as you have to apply your knowledge. Sometimes, exam boards like to recycle questions or use similar wording, so you may get a question you know or is similar to one you have already done on the exam!

Who do I go to if I need support?

There are many people you can go to if you need support! You can talk to family members: parents, siblings, aunties, uncles grandparents. In school, you can talk to any teacher. They are there to help you and support you! If that does not work for you, there are online services such as Kooth

that allow you to talk to trained counsellors or other students! Just remember that you are not alone and there are people to support you!

Sleep Tips

It can be difficult to fall asleep if you're stressed or have an "active mind" (thinking about things). Here are some tips to help you fall asleep faster!

· Try to avoid revising until 2am,

· Try to avoid your phone or any bright screens 30 minutes before bed,

· Sleep with your phone/devices in a separate room so you're not tempted to use it,

· Keep a consistent bedtime,

· Try to avoid caffine later in the day,

· Sometimes a warm shower or bath straight before bed can help you relax.
