Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for A-Level Dance revision. These are resources I found through research as I did not take Dance at A-Level myself. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. AQA Website

AQA's Website is best for knowing exactly what you're being assessed on. Use the specification alongside past papers, mark schemes, examiner reports and exemplar work. AQA specifically provides exemplification materials with examiner commentaries.

2. Rambert Classroom

Rambert Classroom , as mentioned above, is best for professional knowledge, choreography and seeing how professional dancers actually work. It's particularly useful for moving beyond textbook definitions and seeing professional practice in action.

3. Digital Theatre+

I Think Therefore I Teach is best for watching and analysing professional performances. For Dance, actually watching repertoire is a major part of developing critical engagement. Don't just read someone else's analysis of a performance. Watch it yourself and make observations about movement, relationships, space, dynamics, costume, lighting, aural setting and choreographic intention.

Top Tips:

· Know your specification vocabulary. Terms such as action, space, dynamics, relationships, choreographic devices, structure, aural setting, motif and motif development need to become natural parts of your vocabulary.

· Watch professional dance actively. Don't just watch it like entertainment. Pause and identify what you're seeing.

· Film your rehearsals when allowed. Watching yourself back can reveal things you don't notice while dancing.

· Get feedback early.

· Know why you chose each choreographic device.

· Experiment before settling. Try different structures, dynamics, relationships and aural choices before deciding what works.

· Practise writing about movement precisely.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* (or equivalent) in Dance!

· Analyse the effect, not just the feature. Don't write: "The dancers use unison." Instead, push it towards: "The use of unison creates a sense of collective identity, reinforcing..."

· Make your terminology precise. At the highest level, you should be able to distinguish between what the dancers do and how/why the choreographer has manipulated it.

· Use evidence from the actual work. Be specific about moments, movement material, relationships, formations, dynamics, aural choices and production elements.

· Understand the relationship between choreography and performance. A choreographic idea only communicates effectively if the performer interprets and presents it appropriately.

· Make your practical decisions defensible. If someone asks "Why did you do that?", you should be able to explain how the decision supports your artistic intention.

· Use motif development intelligently.

· Practise extended answers under timed conditions.
