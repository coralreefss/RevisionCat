Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for A-Level Design and Technology revision. These are resources I found through research as I did not take DT at A-Level myself. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. Your exam board website

Your exam board's website is best for knowing what you're actually being examined on. Use your specification, past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports. AQA usually provides example student answers with examiner commentaries too.

2. A Level DT

A Level DT is particularly useful for AQA Product Design students, with revision notes and past paper resources organised around the course. The website is free to use and also has a revision tracker and quiz mode!

3. DT Base

DT Base is great for DT revision across AQA, Edexcel and OCR. It provides revision notes, questions, flashcards, past papers, and even coursework A* examples and advice. They also offer tutoring if you're willing to pay for it.

If you're looking for some Youtube channels to help you revise, below are a few I found through research for the A-Level DT exams.

Product Design Online is excellent for product modelling, CAD and design workflows. It also has a range of videos covering 3D printing, a software called Fusion360 and how to use it, including tips and tricks for designing and modelling.

The Engineering Mindset with almost 5 million subscibers, is excellent for understanding the engineering and manufacturing side of DT. Mitul Does DT is made by a DT student at university. There is quite a lot of content and it is great for "last minute tips" and understadnding the NEA work. He also walks through some past papers and has created hour long study session videos. From my research, I would really recommend him.

Top Tips:

· Learn manufacturing processes properly. Know how each process works, which materials it suits, the equipment involved and whether it is appropriate for batch, mass or one-off production.

· Make comparison tables. Compare materials and processes by cost, strength, durability, finish, accuracy, production volume and environmental impact.

· Understand CAD and CAM. Know their advantages, limitations and effects on accuracy, repeatability, productivity and cost.

· Keep a DT vocabulary bank of key terminology (and definitions).

· Practise technical drawings. Develop confidence with orthographic drawings, isometric sketches, exploded diagrams and annotations where relevant to your course.

· Use calculations regularly. Practise any mathematical principles required by your specification, including tolerances, dimensions and relevant mechanical or material calculations.

· For your NEA, document the process. Record design decisions, research, testing, feedback and changes as you go.

· Understand before memorising. If you understand why something happens, the details become much easier to retain.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* in DT!

· Justify every technical decision. Don't simply state that aluminium is suitable; explain how its specific properties meet the product's requirements and why alternatives may be less suitable.

· Use precise terminology.

· Consider the scale of production. A manufacturing method suitable for a prototype might be inefficient or too expensive for mass production.

· Link properties to performance. Explain how material choice affects weight, durability, safety, cost, appearance and product lifespan.

· Practise longer written answers.

· Test your own algorithms. Try normal cases, boundary cases and invalid inputs.

· Practise writing high-scoring answers under time pressure. At A* level, knowing the DT isn't enough if you can't communicate it quickly and precisely.
