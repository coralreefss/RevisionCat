Top Tips:

· Learn the assessment criteria, not just the content. Criminology is different from a traditional A level because Units 1 and 3 are internally assessed and Units 2 and 4 are externally examined.

· Know your case studies very well. Don't just memorise the name of a case. Know what happened, why it is relevant, which concept/theory it demonstrates and what you can use it as evidence for.

· Make theory comparison tables. For every theory, know its assumptions, key concepts, supporting evidence, strengths, weaknesses and how it compares with other theories.

· Practise applying theories to scenarios.

· Keep your evidence organised. A useful structure is: Claim → Evidence → Explanation → Evaluation.

· Keep up with current examples. Crime, policing, punishment and criminal justice are constantly changing, so contemporary examples can make your work much more convincing.

· Make an error log. Record recurring mistakes rather than simply recording your percentage.

· Understand before memorising. If you understand why something happens, the details become much easier to retain.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* (or equivalent) in Criminology!

· Make every point analytical. Don't stop at what. Push yourself to why, how, and so what?

· Build evaluation into your answers.

· Compare theories explicitly. For example, explain how two theories would interpret the same behaviour differently, rather than evaluating each theory separately.

· Use evidence strategically. A case study should prove or illustrate a point, not just sit in your answer because you memorised it.

· Know the limitations of your evidence. Ask whether the case is representative, whether the evidence is reliable, and whether another explanation could account for the same behaviour.

· Use precise criminological terminology.

· Make links between units. The qualification is designed around connected knowledge, and official guidance/resources emphasise drawing knowledge from across the course.

· Use the official exam walkthrough. Eduqas provides an exam walkthrough built around past paper questions specifically to demonstrate how to approach the examination.

· Practise writing mark-scheme-worthy answers under time pressure. At A* level, knowing the Criminology isn't enough if you can't communicate it quickly and precisely.
