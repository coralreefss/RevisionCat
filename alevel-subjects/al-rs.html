Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for A-Level Religious Studies revision. These are resources I found through research as I did not take Religious Studies at A-Level myself. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. Your exam board's website

Your exam board's website (which can be found above) will always be one of the most useful resources. It can tell you exactly what you're being assessed on and what the examiners will be looking for. Use the spec as your master checklist, alongside past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports. Make sure every scholar, argument, belief and issue you revise actually appears on your course.

2. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy is best for when you need to properly understand a philosophical argument rather than just memorise a simplified explanation.

3. Project Gutenberg

Project Gutenberg may be useful for wider reading or learning more about philosophers or scholars. All texts are free, easy to access and sorted by topics. There are also many other texts involving History or Medicine if that is something you may also be interested in.

YouTube Channels:

If you're looking for some Youtube channels to help you revise, below are a few I found through research for the A-Level Religious Studies exams.

1. Wireless Philosophy (Wi-Phi)

Wireless Philosophy (Wi-Phi) is excellent for short, clear explanations of philosophical ideas. The channel is ran by "professors from Yale, Stanford, Oxford, MIT, and more!" They provide over 130 animated videos dicussing different topics such as ethics and epistemology.

2. CrashCourse Philosophy

CrashCourse Philosophy provides great introductions to major philosophical concepts. Their videos are relatively short and very easy to understand. This channel could be very beneifical for understanding concepts and ideas.

3. Philosophy Tube

Philosophy Tube provides videos about discussions of philosophical and ethical issues such as "Mad Marx" and "what was liberalism".

4. Closer To Truth

Closer To Truth may be excellent for philosophy of religion and questions about existence. Their videos dicuss a main question and often include special guests. There are also dicsussions on Global Philosophy of Religion Project and on the science of consciousness which may be interesting or beneficial for you.

5. Then & Now

Then & Now is great for philosophy, history and intellectual context. Their videos are a tad longer but may provide more detailed insight. There are over 100 videos on Philosophy that you may find helpful!

Top Tips:

· Learn arguments, not just conclusions. Know the premises, reasoning, conclusion and assumptions behind each argument.

· Build argument chains.

· Create scholar comparison tables.

· Learn religious teachings in context. Don't just memorise quotations; understand what they mean and how they relate to wider beliefs.

· Make an error log. Record recurring mistakes rather than simply recording your percentage.

· Know different interpretations within religions. Avoid treating a religion as if every believer has exactly the same viewpoint.

· Debate out loud. Pick a statement and argue both sides without looking at your notes.

· Make links between topics. RS becomes much easier when you can connect ideas rather than treating every topic separately.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* in Religious Studies!

· Make evaluation specific. Instead of saying an argument is “weak”, explain exactly why the reasoning is problematic.

· Evaluate the argument, not the scholar. Identify weaknesses in their reasoning rather than simply saying they're wrong.

· Develop counterarguments.

· Use philosophical terminology accurately.

· Consider implications. Ask what accepting an argument would actually mean for religion, morality or society.

· Use synoptic links. Connect philosophy, ethics and religious thought where relevant.

· Answer the exact question. Don't write everything you know about a topic if it isn't directly relevant.
