Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for A-Level Computer Science revision. These are resources I found through research as I did not take Computer Science at A-Level myself (unfortunately). You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. Your exam board website

Your exam board's website is best for knowing what you're actually being examined on. Use your specification, past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports. AQA usually provides example student answers with examiner commentaries too.

2. Craig 'n' Dave

Craig 'n' Dave is absolutely brilliant for OCR-specific theory revision. Their videos are short enough to work through topic-by-topic and are particularly useful when you need someone to explain a difficult concept rather than just read a textbook definition.

3. W3Schools

W3Schools is great for A-level Computer Science revision, specifically for programming. It is a free online platform that provides interactive lessons and quizzes on various topics, as well as just information on different programming languages. It is a great way to test your knowledge and reinforce your understanding of the subject, or look up specific words, commands or syntax.

YouTube Channels:

If you're looking for some Youtube channels to help you revise, below are a few I found through research for the A-Level Computer Science exams.

1. Craig 'n' Dave

Craig 'n' Dave is excellent for A-level Computer Science explanations and exam technique. They mainly cover OCR content, but most topics will be relevant to other exam boards too. They also have a website with more resources and revision materials.

2. Computerphile

Computerphile provides deeper explanations of real-world Computer Science concepts beyond the spec. There are many special guests and also videos covering specific topics such as AI.

3. Mr Brown CS

Mr Brown CS is made by an assistant principal at a London Secondary School. He may be useful for Computer Science explanations and exam technique, specifically for OCR content, but most topics will be relevant to other exam boards too. All of his videos are covered by topic and separated into playlists.

4. freeCodeCamp.org

freeCodeCamp.org with over 11 million subscribers, is excellent for deeper conceptual explanations and programming fundamentals. There is a huge range of programming and Computer Science tutorials. It is technically beyond the course content, but worth trying if you're interested in programming or want to sharpen your skills!

Top Tips:

· Know the specification inside out. A-level Computer Science has a LOT of small details that are easy to overlook.

· Program regularly. Programming is a skill; reading code isn't enough.

· Practise writing algorithms on paper. Exams won't always give you an IDE to test your solution.

· Know your data structures. Understand arrays, records, stacks, queues, linked lists, trees, graphs and hash tables rather than simply memorising their names.

· Make an error log. Record recurring mistakes rather than simply recording your percentage.

· Practise hexadecimal and binary conversions regularly. They're quick marks when you're confident and annoying marks to lose when you're not.

· Practise SQL. Write queries yourself instead of only reading examples.

· Understand before memorising. If you understand why something happens, the details become much easier to retain.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* in Computer Science!

· Think in terms of processes. For example, don't just know that TCP provides reliable communication — understand how reliability is achieved.

· Use precise terminology.

· Know your complexity comparisons. Be able to explain why an algorithm is more or less efficient,

· Understand abstraction and decomposition deeply. These are fundamental computational thinking skills that appear throughout the course.

· Practise longer written answers.

· Test your own algorithms. Try normal cases, boundary cases and invalid inputs.

· Practise writing high-scoring answers under time pressure. At A* level, knowing the Computer Science isn't enough if you can't communicate it quickly and precisely.
