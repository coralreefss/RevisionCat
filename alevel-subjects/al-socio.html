Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for A-Level Sociology revision. These are resources I found through research as I did not take Sociology at A-Level myself. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. Your exam board's website

Your exam board's website (which can be found above) will always be one of the most useful resources. It can tell you exactly what you're being assessed on and what the examiners will be looking for. Use the spec alongside past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports.

2. The Sociology Teacher

The Sociology Teacher is one of the best websites I could find for this subject. It provides a range of resources, including revision methods, exam objectives and typical questions, and explainations of key concepts and theories, all of which are sectioned by topic.

3. ReviseSociology

ReviseSociology is second best to The Sociology Teacher. It focuses on exam technique and provides key info about each paper and (AQA) and goes through each typical question. Separately, you can pay for other resources if you want to, but the free resources are excellent for revision, such as the introduction to Sociology.

YouTube Channels:

If you're looking for some Youtube channels to help you revise, below are a few I found through research for the A-Level Sociology exams.

1. Tutor2u

Tutor2u is excellent for explanations, revision sessions and exam technique.

2. The TEACHER SOCIOLOGY

The TEACHER SOCIOLOGY provides topic explanations and revision advice. There are many videos on A* level content and concepts, and even walkthroughs of papers with student examples. Additionally, there is a 5-a-day challenge. I personally found this technique very useful in maths, so you may find it works well for Sociology too!

3. The Sociology Guy

The Sociology Guy is very useful for content revision and exam technique. He may not be the best resource for all topics, but he provides some videos on sociological theories and concepts that could be very useful for revision.

4. CrashCourse Sociology

CrashCourse Sociology are excellent for revision. They provide a range of videos on many concepts, including what sociology actually is, research methods and individual topics. The videos are often animated and easy to follow.

5. Sociology Live!

Sociology Live! provides discussions and explanations of sociological issues. There are a few videos on different sociological topics, and the channel is useful for understanding how to apply sociological concepts to real life. However, this channel will not help you with general revision.

Top Tips:

· Learn theories alongside topics.

·Keep contemporary evidence. Sociology focuses on contemporary society, so statistics, recent studies and current examples can make your answers much stronger.

· Learn research methods properly. Don't just memorise definitions. Know why a method might be suitable for a particular research question.

· Know the strengths AND weaknesses of methods.

· Use key concepts and terminology precisely.

· Make “evidence cards”. One side = claim/theory. Other side = statistic, study or contemporary example that supports it.

· Practise application.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* in Sociology!

·Go beyond “this supports/criticises”. Explain exactly why the evidence strengthens or weakens the argument.

· Make evaluation comparative.

· Use evidence strategically. One highly relevant study or statistic is much better than throwing five vaguely related names into an essay.

· Challenge assumptions. Ask what a theory assumes about society, individuals, power, culture or human behaviour.

· Make every paragraph answer the question. Your final sentence should ideally bring the paragraph back to the exact wording of the question.

· Have a judgement ready for essay questions. You don't necessarily need to decide that one theory is completely correct; you can make a nuanced judgement about which explanation is more convincing under particular circumstances.
