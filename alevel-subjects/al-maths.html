Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for A-Level Maths revision. These are resources I found both through research and my own personal recommendations as I am currently taking Maths Alevel. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. Physics & Maths Tutor (PMT)

PMT is one of the best resources for organised, exam-board-specific practice. Work through questions by topic, then use the worked solutions to identify exactly where your method went wrong. They also provide past papers and cheat sheets which may be very useful.

2. TLMaths

BFI Education Brilliant when you need a concept explained step by step. Use the videos to learn a method, then immediately attempt questions without looking at the solution. He also has a google document containing links to all the teaching videos he's made, which are short and easy to understand. TLMaths is definitely one of the best resources.

3. Yesterday's Maths Exam

Yesterday's Maths Exam is particularly useful for past papers and course information. Covering AQA, Edexcel and OCR at A-level and Further Maths levels, this website will be so useful for quick access to resources. It is easy to access and a website I will be using a lot more myself.

Top Tips:

· Practise Maths every week, or as often as you can. Regular problem-solving is much more effective than leaving a huge amount of practice until an assessment.

· Attempt questions before watching solutions.

· Keep an error log. Record the topic, the mistake, why it happened and what you should do differently next time.

· Show every important step. Clear working helps you spot mistakes and can earn method marks even if your final answer is wrong.

· Use mixed-topic questions. Once you've learnt a topic, practise identifying it when it appears alongside unfamiliar material.

· Learn your calculator properly

· Use the specification as a checklist. Make sure you cover Pure, Statistics and Mechanics rather than focusing exclusively on Pure.

Practise under timed conditions. Speed matters, but accuracy and choosing the correct method matter just as much.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* in Maths!

· Recognise hidden structures. A difficult question may combine familiar techniques such as substitution, factorisation, differentiation and trigonometric identities.

· Develop flexible problem-solving. If your first method fails, consider another approach r ather than repeatedly trying the same thing.

· Practise unfamiliar questions. The hardest questions often test whether you can combine known ideas in a new context.

· Use exact values where appropriate. Avoid unnecessary rounding during working, especially when exact answers are expected. Leaving an answer in surd or fraction form until the end of a question is okay!

· Practise complete papers.

· Explain your reasoning clearly. If a question says “show that”, make sure your working demonstrates the required result rather than simply stating it.

· Prioritise weak topics strategically. Spend time on areas that are both important and currently costing you marks.
