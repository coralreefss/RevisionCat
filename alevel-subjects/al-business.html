Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for A-Level Business revision. These are resources I found both through research and sites I use myself as I am currently taking Business at A-Level. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. Your exam board website

Your exam board's website is best for knowing what you're actually being examined on. Use your specification, current CSP list, past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports. AQA explicitly provides example student answers with examiner commentaries too.

2. Tutor2u Business

Tutor2u Business is probably the best all-round revision resource. It has topic videos, study notes, quizzes, practice questions, case studies and exam support, and it currently has resources specifically aligned to different exam boards: AQA and Edexcel. There are also livestreams almost every Wednesday that focus on different aspects of the course. However, these are available even if you miss the livestream.

3. BBC News Business

BBC News Business is not best for actual revision, but it's great for staying updated with current business news. Use it to build a bank of real businesses, real events and real data that you can apply to exam questions. Or you could apply current world news events to your Business knowledge to help you understand the concepts better. For example, if there is a Partnership business that is making redundancies, you could apply your knowledge of the Partnership business structure to explain why this is happening (cutting costs) and what the consequences are (higher unemployment rate).

If you're looking for some Youtube channels to help you revise, below are a few I found through research, and recommendations of channels I am using myself, for the A-Level Business exams.

Tutor2u is excellent for A-level Business explanations and exam technique. They cover a range of exam boards and have Live Revision videos and recorded videos on a range of topics. They also have quizzes and practice questions. I recommend them the most out of all resources.

TakingTheBiz focuses specifically on A-level Business content and exam questions. There are also quizzes and required practicals. Bizconsesh provides topic explanations and exam technique support. He covers a wide range of exam boards that are sectioned by theme or topic in the playlists. Academic Plus is another channel that provides videos across multiple exam boards. He has a range of videos on all the themes and exam technique. There are a mix of shorts providing quick questions and longer explaination videos. He also provides tutoring that you can pay for.

Top Tips:

· Start with the specification. Use it to identify exactly what you need to know.

· Learn the definition AND the application.

· Know your calculations completely and practice them often. Don't just memorise formulas. Instead, practise interpreting what the answer actually means for the business.

· Learn the difference between analysis and evaluation. Analysis explains the chain of consequences while evaluation makes a justified judgement.

· Know the difference between objectives. Maximising profit or market share, growth, survival and ethical objectives can lead to very different decisions.

· Learn the advantages AND disadvantages of strategies. Aim for 3 of each.

· Practise interpreting graphs and data.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* in Business!

· Make your application specific. Application is worth a lot of marks in Business. The best way to apply is to use quotations from the case study, numerial statistics or calculations, or use "such as seasoning" (e.g. Nike's competitors, such as Adidas and Puma.)

· Use the case study throughout the answer.

· Think about time. A decision that works in the short term may have very different consequences in the long term.

· Use quantitative evidence. If the case gives you numbers, calculate something useful and use the result in your judgement.

· Think like the manager. Ask yourself: If I were actually running this business, what would I choose and why?

· Practise writing high-scoring answers under time pressure. At A* level, knowing the Business isn't enough if you can't communicate it quickly and precisely.
