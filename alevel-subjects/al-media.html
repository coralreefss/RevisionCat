Specifications:

Below, you can find the specifications for the A-Level Media course. You can find the specification for your exam board by clicking the link below! The specification contains all the information you could be tested on. Use this as a checklist (for all the information you should know for the exams.) If it's not on the specification, you don't need to know it!

Please also check your exact specification with your teacher, as some courses may have many different specifications with slightly differing content.

NOTE: If you're doing AQA, do not revise CSPs from a random online list and assume they're current. AQA says its CSPs are reviewed and updated, with a new CSP booklet published each year. The CSPs are a vehicle for applying the theoretical framework and media contexts, rather than something you're supposed to memorise as isolated products.

Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for A-Level Media revision. These are resources I found through research as I did not take Media at A-Level myself. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. Your exam board website

Your exam board's website is best for knowing what you're actually being examined on. Use your specification, current CSP list, past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports. AQA explicitly provides example student answers with examiner commentaries too.

2. The Media Insider

The Media Insider , as mentioned earlier, is useful for understanding terminology, theories and difficult Media Studies concepts without having to dig through a massive textbook.

3. Media Know All

Media Know All is great for building your theoretical knowledge and finding explanations and examples you can then apply to your CSPs. BUT for current examples, always check that the information is still relevant to your course and use your exam board's specification as the final authority.

Top Tips:

· Start with the specification. A-level Media is too big to revise by simply reading a textbook from beginning to end. Use the specification to identify exactly what you need to know.

· Learn theories through application.

· Annotate the actual products. Screenshot important frames, headlines, layouts, social-media posts etc... and annotate them.

· Know your terminology.

· Make an error log. Record recurring mistakes rather than simply recording your percentage.

· Keep up with contemporary media. Media changes constantly, particularly online platforms, ownership, technology and audience behaviour.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* in Media!

· Memorise command words (describe, state, explain). These often define how you should answer a question. For example, if a question asks you to "describe" something, you should not just state a fact, but instead describe the process or concept in detail.

· Answer the question, not the topic. If the question asks why a particular result occurred, don't dump everything you know about that biological process.

· Practise unseen analysis. Don't only revise your CSPs. A-level exams can give you unfamiliar media products that you need to analyse using your theoretical knowledge.

· Plan essays before writing them. A quick argument/evidence/theory structure can save loads of time.

· Challenge your own interpretation. Ask: Is there another way the audience could read this? This is particularly useful when discussing audience theories.

· Use synoptic links. Connect media language, representation, industries and audiences rather than treating them as four completely separate topics.

· Practise writing high-scoring answers under time pressure. At A* level, knowing the Media isn't enough if you can't communicate it quickly and precisely.
