Top 3 Resources:

Below, you can find my top 3 resources for A-Level Biology revision. These are resources I found through research as I did not take Biology at A-Level myself. You may find other resources that are more useful for your revision!

1. Cognito

Cognito is my personal favourite website for Biology (and science in general). It has (short) animated lessons and quizzes to help you learn and understand content. You can access this through Youtube or Cognito's Website.

2. Physics and Maths Tutor (PMT)

PMT provides comprehensive notes, practice questions and video tutorials to help you understand the concepts. There are also an array of past papers.

3. Seneca

Seneca Best for quick active recall, especially when you don't have the energy to sit and read through pages of notes.

Top Tips:

· Start with the specification. A-level Biology is too big to revise by simply reading a textbook from beginning to end. Use the specification to identify exactly what you need to know.

· Learn processes as chains of cause and effect. For example: stimulus → receptor → coordinator → effector → response.

· Know your definitions precisely. Biology mark schemes can be very particular about terminology.

· Practise graphs and calculations separately.

· Make an error log. Record recurring mistakes rather than simply recording your percentage.

· Understand before memorising. If you understand why something happens, the details become much easier to retain.

· Always mark or ask your teachers to mark your practice questions. If you went wrong, understand why and learn from your mistakes.

A* Tips:

Below, you can find a few tips to help you achieve an A or A* in Biology!

· Memorise command words (describe, state, explain). These often define how you should answer a question. For example, if a question asks you to "describe" something, you should not just state a fact, but instead describe the process or concept in detail.

· Answer the question, not the topic. If the question asks why a particular result occurred, don't dump everything you know about that biological process.

· Use the information given. If a question provides data, an experimental setup or a biological scenario, explicitly connect your biological knowledge to it.

· Become obsessive about "because". For every biological statement, ask yourself: why? And what does that cause next?

· Understand correlation vs causation. Especially in ecology, epidemiology and data-analysis questions.

· Make synoptic links. A-level Biology specifications deliberately connect different areas of Biology. OCR, for example, explicitly includes synoptic assessment across its three written papers.

· Practise writing mark-scheme-worthy answers under time pressure. At A* level, knowing the Biology isn't enough if you can't communicate it quickly and precisely.
